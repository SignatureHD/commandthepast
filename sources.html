— Methodology

Sources & Methodology

How we research, verify, and present historical content.

01 / Statement

The three types of content

Command The Past uses a structured research process that separates three types of content. Each is presented and labelled differently to keep historical record and interactive interpretation cleanly distinct.

01

Verified historical record

Facts, dates, names, and outcomes that are confirmed by multiple cited primary and secondary sources. Shown without qualification.

02

Probable or estimated

Information that is consistent across most sources but where exact figures or details vary. Clearly labelled with confidence badges.

03

Interactive interpretation

Game scenarios and alternate decision models. These are educational tools — not historical claims. They are clearly framed as such throughout the site.

02 / Sources

Primary research sources

Eleven sources form the verification backbone for the Spion Kop entry. The same standards apply to every future battle.

— Reference · Open

South African History Online (SAHO)

Comprehensive South African historical reference. Used for context, narrative, and cross-reference.

— Archival · Official

NARSSA / NAAIRS

National Archives and Records Service of South Africa — the primary official verification route for South African military and archival records. Key source for casualty figures and order of battle.

— Peer-reviewed

Military History Journal / SAMHS

South African Military History Society. Peer-reviewed historical journal covering Anglo-Boer War and other South African conflicts in depth.

— Reference · Specialist

AngloBoerWar.com

Detailed reference for unit histories, personal accounts, and battle chronology. Cross-referenced against other sources.

— Secondary · Definitive

Pakenham, Thomas — The Boer War

Weidenfeld & Nicolson, 1979. The most comprehensive single-volume account of the war in English. Used as a primary secondary source throughout.

— Primary · Contemporary

Churchill, Winston — London to Ladysmith via Pretoria

1900. Eyewitness account by Churchill as war correspondent. Valuable for atmosphere and command atmosphere. Used with awareness of Churchill's perspective and political interests.

— Primary · Contemporary

Doyle, Arthur Conan — The Great Boer War

1900. Contemporary account by Doyle, who served as a medical volunteer. Useful for period detail.

— Cartographic

Stanford Africana Historical Maps

Historical map collection used for terrain and campaign geography research.

— Cartographic · Imperial

Canadian War Museum — Boer War collections

Map and archival holdings relevant to British and Imperial forces.

— Regional · Site-based

KwaZulu-Natal Battlefields Route

Regional historical resource for terrain, site visits, and local historical knowledge.

— Archival · Digital

National Library of South Africa — Digital Collections

Digital archive for period documents, maps, and photographs.

03 / Framework

How we classify historical certainty

Four labels, applied consistently across every battle page, narrative section, and data field.

verified
Confirmed by multiple primary or peer-reviewed sources. Used with confidence.
probable
Consistent across most secondary sources; primary verification recommended before final publication.
contested
Sources disagree meaningfully. Figures or facts are presented as estimates with the disagreement noted.
unknown
Insufficient reliable data. Presented as unknown rather than guessed.

Casualty figures for Spion Kop fall primarily in the contested category. Different sources — British, Boer, and later historians — count differently and count different things. We present ranges and estimates, not false precision.

05 / Policy

What we do and do not do

Four standing rules that govern how the site presents historical material.

— Rule 01 · Citation

We do not copy long passages from copyrighted sources. We do not reproduce primary source documents without rights clearance. We summarise and attribute.

— Rule 02 · Interpretation

We do not invent or speculate beyond what sources support. Where we present alternate decision scenarios in the game, we frame them explicitly as educational interpretation — not historical fact.

— Rule 03 · Perspective

We do not present one belligerent's account as the objective truth. The Anglo-Boer War is remembered differently in different communities — British, Afrikaner, Black South African, and Indian South African. All perspectives deserve acknowledgement.

— Rule 04 · Versioning

As verified data replaces estimated data, confidence badges and source notes will be updated. Version dates are included in the data model. The site is a living document.
